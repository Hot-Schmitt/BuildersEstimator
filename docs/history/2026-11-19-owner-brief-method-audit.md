# The owner's brief for the method audit, Nov 19, 2026

**Purpose:** the owner's own words asking for a check of how this project is set up before the design work goes further. Kept so the audit, and anyone judging it later, can be held against what he asked for. Dictated; lightly trimmed for transcription slips, nothing added. "[the builder]" replaces a name.

## What he asked for

> I want you to take a look at yourself, your code, where the code's stored, how the files are, your logic, agents that you use. Research, complaints or praises about other estimating apps. Unbias yourself to determine if you have all the right pieces and setup to create, design, map out, what a construction estimating app for a builder like [the builder] or one that has fewer than 5 employees needs to have, needs to look at. What do they ask for? How can your app be better?

> Not necessarily on the fully automated picking the prices for them, because some builders might not like that. Their industry knowledge and their own pricing would probably be [better than] anything that we can come up with. ... the estimating is to get as close as possible and help them.

> I just want to make sure that you're storing documents right, that your code, your rules, steps that you have to take, when to push back, etc. How we move through each phase of this project so that when we get to screens, all the buttons have been mapped. There's logic behind every user story. Every field, every place on the software has a well-documented note that you can easily access, or have agents access when needed, that you know when to use certain things and when not to use certain things.

> That when we get to the mock screens, designers or any other agents are able to craft something truly remarkable. Back-end, front-end developers don't make decisions. They just say yes or no on feasibility or what is complicated and adds build time and so on.

> That you fully thought through your methodology, not biasing yourself to what I may have asked, not being as well-versed in methodology for building something like this, that you know which file types need to be stored. One of my friends mentioned JSONB file types for something because it makes it faster and easier to read. That's just an example. I'm not telling you to do that.

> The goal for me with how well you work is that we don't have to do a lot of back and forth, and by the time we get done locking some mock screens, every button's been mapped, all logic has been tested, we know what every button does, what it doesn't do, we know what every field is, and its back end has been planned. We know what every screen is. We know how to work through an estimate. We've looked at edge cases that might break the system. We have considered at least a few options ... for some of the features, in terms of how it will be done in the build phase.

> Basically, so well documented after everything or every step is locked ... that when we get to the build phase, the document is already structured and there's already so much information from our work that it makes the build phase go so much faster.

> Research yourself, research frameworks and methodologies for building this. Double check your research on estimation apps. The good, the bad. Make sure you consult with high quality builder agents that can, one, tell you what they like to have, and then also do blind reads on the mock screens. To give feedback on if it's intuitive, does it value them? Do they love it? Do they just like it?
