This page quotes figures from his Job A estimate, so it is not opened during a blind run on Job A.

## Needed first
1. **What does "80% done" mean to you?** Which parts do you want filled in, and which do you always do yourself?
2. **What do you hand a new job in as?** Full plans, a sketch, a walk-through and notes, photos? Does it differ for renovations?
3. **Are the 2022 prices in your sheet still what you use?** If not, is there a recent estimate we can take current prices from?
4. **Can you share two to four more finished jobs?** For each: plans, your estimate, and the cost sheet. Ideally one renovation, one full new build, one more ADU or garage.
5. **Do you keep a blank master estimate sheet?** Or do you copy the last job each time?
6. **Which lines are always a sub's bid?** (Electrical, plumbing, HVAC, drywall, roofing looked like bids on Job A.) Do you get bids before or after the estimate goes out?

## About how you price
7. Where do your unit prices come from: memory, last invoice, a supplier quote? How often do you refresh them?
8. Which categories do you trust your sheet on, and which do you always adjust by feel?
9. Is overhead and profit always 25%? Does it change by job type or size? (On Job A it is exactly 25% of cost.)
10. Do you add a contingency or cushion? Where does it go: inside line prices, a separate line, or the markup? (On Job A: 20% on framing material and 33% on siding material, plus 8.9% tax on both. Are those standing rates?)
11. General conditions on Job A were estimated at about 10,300 and came in near 4,800. Is that line a deliberate cushion?
12. How do you handle items the client has not chosen yet (tile, fixtures, cabinets)? Allowance amounts?

## About which line you use (added Nov 16, from the first blind draft)
Your sheet often has two or three lines for the same work. We need to know which one you use.
30. On a garage apartment, which line carries electrical: your main electrical bid, the apartment bid, or both? (On Job A the whole job is on the main bid line.)
31. Is framing labor always one lump sum on the "Framing - labor" line? What do you base it on? (On Job A the per-square-foot lines under it are empty.)
32. "Plumbing install" shows a count of 10 on Job A. Is that a count of fixtures? Which fixtures count? When do you use the "Plumbing bid" line?
38. For a small wet bar, do you list the cabinets piece by piece, and which counter line do you use? (On Job A the top is on the laundry counter line.)

## About quantities
13. Which quantities do you measure off the plans yourself, and which come from a supplier (lumber list, window quote)?
14. What waste do you add (lumber, drywall, tile, siding)?
15. What are your rules of thumb, for example dumpsters per job, months of toilet rental, paint per square foot?
22. **(Added Nov 16, from the plan-reading test)** Your slab concrete on Job A uses a 2 ft × 1 ft turn-down with a 45° flare, plus 40 ft of grade beam. The plans show a 12" minimum turn-down. Is your footing size a standing rule for slabs? Where was the grade beam?
23. **(Added Nov 16)** Siding on Job A works out to net wall area plus 7%, at 7 sf per plank. Is that your rule?
24. **(Added Nov 16; gutters removed the same day, since ogee and K-style are most likely the same profile)** Where the plans and your estimate differ, which wins? On Job A: two exterior doors drawn 2'8" wide, priced 2'6"; porch rafters drawn 2×6, priced 2×8.
25. **(Added Nov 16)** Do you use the lumber supplier's takeoff for framing, or your own counts? (On Job A they differ a lot.)
33. **(Added Nov 16, from the first blind draft)** Treated 2x4x16 shows 85 on Job A, the same as the 2x4x8 studs on the next line. Is that right?
34. **(Added Nov 16)** How much extra do you order over the plan count on studs, plates, rafters, sheathing and trim boards? (On Job A your counts run about 15 to 45% over the bare plan count; rebar and concrete far more.)
35. **(Added Nov 16)** Job A has 3 CY of driveway concrete and 800 sf of driveway demo. Neither is on the plans. Where did they come from?
36. **(Added Nov 16)** The permit line has no price on Job A. Did the owner pay for it?
37. **(Added Nov 16)** Two water heater lines are priced on Job A. Are both needed?

## About cost codes
26. **(Added Nov 16, corrected the same day)** Your cost sheet keeps Labor, Material and Sub under each code. On Job A it looks like you file by who does the work: a sub's work goes to Sub even when your estimate had it in the Labor column (framing labor, tile labor). Is that the rule? When does something go to Labor instead?
27. **(Added Nov 16)** Is the code list in your Job A cost sheet (93 codes) your standing list for every job?
28. **(Added Nov 16)** Your Job A estimate lists "Install cabinets, 2 days at 350" but the sheet shows no cost for it, and your cost sheet budgets it at 700. Is that a slip in the sheet?
29. **(Added Nov 16)** Your one paint line (12,000) was budgeted half to interior and half to exterior paint. Is that split a habit or a guess for that job? (The user's reading: one sub quote for the whole house, halved so each code had a figure.)

## About renovations

17. What surprises have cost you the most on old houses?

## About the result
18. Must the draft come back as an Excel file in exactly your layout?
19. Would colored cells (green, yellow, red) for confidence help or annoy you? Where would you want the "check these first" list?
20. When you change a number, would you add a two-word reason ("price up", "missed it", "client wants")? Or should we work it out from the change alone?
21. What is your usual time to produce an estimate for a job like Job A?

43. Are there costs you pass through without marking them up (permit fees, for example)?
44. Which groups of line items carry contingency as a standing habit? Does the contingency stay on once a real price is in? (Decision 0004.)

## About what the client gets, bids and changes (added Nov 20, from the full review)
The wording is Claude's (Proposed); the facts they ask for are his. Each names the review finding it came from (`docs/history/review-findings.md`).
49. What do you hand a client today as the estimate: your sheet, a summary, a proposal, a contract? What does the client see and not see (line detail, your markup, exclusions, terms)? How do they sign? (FR-2.1)
50. Do you price alternates (an add or a deduct the client can choose)? How do you show them? (FR-2.5)
51. How many bids do you usually get for one trade? Do you keep track of what each bid includes, leaves out, and how long it is good for? (FR-2.4, FR-3.9)
52. When an estimate changes after the client has seen it, what do you do: a new estimate, a change order, a marked-up copy? How does the client see what changed? (FR-2.5)
53. The 20% on framing material and the 33% on siding material on Job A: what is each one for? Waste, a cushion for prices moving, or cover for surprises? Is it on top of the extra you already order over the plan count? (FR-3.3; goes with questions 10, 34 and 44.)
54. Do you price general conditions from how long the job will run? How do you judge the length of a job? (FR-3.10c; goes with questions 11 and 15.)
