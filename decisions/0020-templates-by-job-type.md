## Decision

A draft starts from a template of the builder's line items for that kind of job, not
from all 471 of his lines. The first template is built from what he used on the Job A
garage apartment.

- He can add a line item for one job only, save his changes as a new template, and set
  any template as his default.
- Templates can be combined into a new one.
- "Create new" starts from his full list or a blank one and is trimmed inside the
  estimate.
- "Update this template" asks first, shows what it adds and removes, and counts only his
  own changes. After combining, only "Save as new template" is offered.
- The setup screen only sets up the estimate. He chooses a template and creates the
  estimate; line items are added, and templates saved or updated, inside the estimate.

## Why

Starting every job from his whole list left 183 lines on Job A marked Ask with the same
blanket reason, which buried the 82 real questions. A template per job type answers most
of them before he sees the draft.

## Considered instead

**Every draft starts from all of his lines.** How the Job A remake worked; rule-
following but too many questions for him to answer.

**"Create new" as roughly 470 tick boxes on the setup screen.** A review finding;
replaced by starting from the full list or a blank one and trimming inside the estimate.

**Per-job questions and per-line settings on the setup screen.** Dropped; see decision
0021.

## What it costs us

The first template can only mirror what he used on Job A. Which line items truly vary by
job is a guess from one job until the builder or more finished jobs settle it. Templates
collided with the never-drop-a-line rule; decision 0021 is the fix.
