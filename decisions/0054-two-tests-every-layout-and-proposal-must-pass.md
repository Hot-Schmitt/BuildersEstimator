## Decision

**The layout test (2026-11-16).** He can easily understand where things are and how to
add things; he can jump back to parts of the estimate; it is intuitive for someone about
50; he can drill down when needed and does not have to look for items when moving to the
next section. In practice, five tasks done with no help: find a line item, change a
price, add one, find what needs pricing, get back to the total.

**The standard for every proposal (2026-11-18).** Not overly complicated, yet still lets
the estimator be very accurate; simple on the surface, intuitive, and not busy for a man
of about 50.

## Why

The owner set both, in the words below. They are what a proposal or a drawing is judged
against before it reaches him.

## Considered instead

None recorded; these are the owner's own tests. What they replaced was Claude judging a
layout by eye: its own second laptop sketch failed three of the five tasks when a
reviewer walked them.

## What it costs us

Neither test can be passed on paper. Whether a screen feels simple to him is settled by
drawing at real size, the two-lane review, and the five tasks on a clickable version
(decision 0011). Accuracy and simplicity pull against each other, and this standard asks
for both.

## His words

> Users able to easily understand where things are, how to add things. How to jump back
> to certain parts of the estimate. Was it intuitive for someone who's 50 years old?
> Does it allow user to drill down when needed, not have to look for certain items when
> he's moving on to the next section?

> Make sure that what you're proposing isn't overly complicated, yet still allows this
> estimator to be incredibly accurate... will appear simple on the surface, intuitive to
> use, and not busy for a 50-year-old man to use
