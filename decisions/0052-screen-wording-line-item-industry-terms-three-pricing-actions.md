## Decision

- The word is "line item", never "row".
- "Quote" is never used for vendor or sub pricing, because it can mean either side.
- "Estimated range", not "likely range".
- Every word on a screen is the term a builder or mainstream estimating software uses,
  or close to it, checked against a dated source and listed in a wording table.
- A sketch holds only real screen text. Notes to the owner go in chat. One spacing scale
  and one button layout rule.
- The three pricing actions are **Request pricing** (emails vendors and subs), **Add
  vendor or sub pricing** (a pricing document received) and **Enter price** (he types
  his own number).

## Why

The owner found "row" and "quote" ambiguous, did not recognise the project's own words
on a sketch, and could not tell three similar pricing actions apart.

## Considered instead

**"Add a quote" / "Add supplier estimate".** The owner's own example was "add supplier
estimate"; a reviewer noted "estimate" collides with his own estimate, so Claude chose
"Add vendor or sub pricing".

**"Add pricing" on the price cell.** Locked at 10:45 pm on 2026-11-16 and renamed "Enter
price" the next day, to tell it apart from the other two.

**Project terms on screen ("plan minimum", "Medium"), and a note to the owner inside a
drawing.** Both happened; the second was the same kind of mistake twice, which is why
this became a rule.

## What it costs us

Every label needs a dated source, and that table is not complete: no source was opened
for the design document's first pass. One product's own term for a pricing request uses
the word ruled out here; "Request pricing" stands.
