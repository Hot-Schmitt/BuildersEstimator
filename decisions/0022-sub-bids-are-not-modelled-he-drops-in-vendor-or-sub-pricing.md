## Decision

Subcontractor bids are not modelled from his sheet. A sub bid line stays blank and
marked for him, with the last known figure beside it.

Instead, he drops a pricing document he received (a vendor's material pricing or a sub's
estimate) into the working estimate. The tool says what it read and suggests which line
items it belongs to; he confirms, moves it to a different line item, or splits it.

## Why

A sub's bid depends on the sub and the job, not on anything in the builder's
spreadsheet, so a modelled figure would be a made-up number. Dropping in the real
document is fast and keeps every price his.

## Considered instead

**Modelling sub bids from his past sheet.** Rejected by the owner in the words below.

**Leaving the drop-in for later.** It was parked on the morning of 2026-11-16 as a
strong time saver to schedule later, and included that afternoon.

## What it costs us

A large share of an estimate's dollars stays blank until pricing arrives, so a first
draft is less complete than "80% done" suggests on jobs heavy with subs. Which of his
lines are sub bids or allowances is a fact only he knows.

## His words

> anything that's a subcontractor bid is most likely always going to be impossible to
> model... drop a vendor's pricing or a subcontractor's estimate into the working bid
> and have him confirm which rows
